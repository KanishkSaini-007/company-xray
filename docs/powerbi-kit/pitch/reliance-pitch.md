# Acquire Reliance Industries (RELIANCE.NS) - the case built on Oil & Gas

**Target:** Reliance Industries (NSE: RELIANCE)
**Price:** ~Rs 1,211 (Oct 16, 2026), market cap ~Rs 16.3 lakh crore
**The weak spot:** Oil & Gas - Rs 6,298 crore revenue in Q1 FY27, the smallest segment, growing just +3.2% YoY with EBITDA flat (-0.5%). KGD6 gas production is declining.

## The pitch in 5 bullets
1. The franchise: Jio (Digital Services) grew revenue +12% with a 53.3% EBITDA margin and 533M subscribers; Retail did Rs 90,408 crore in one quarter. Consumer businesses now deliver over half of consolidated EBITDA. This is a consumer-tech company still priced partly like a refinery.
2. The weak spot is fixable: O&G is small (under 2% of revenue) and structurally shrinking on KGD6 decline. O2C margins (8.4%) are cyclical and whipsawed by crude - Q1's +30% revenue was mostly oil price, not volume.
3. What the buyer changes: (a) separate or sell the O&G and O2C energy assets, (b) complete the announced Jio IPO to force a market price on the best asset, (c) keep Retail's digital commerce investment funded from Jio's cash flow.
4. Why now: Jio's IPO is already filed (DRHP mentioned in the Q1 FY27 release). A buyer ahead of the IPO repricing gets the energy drag at today's conglomerate discount.
5. The math: strip out O2C+O&G and the remaining Jio+Retail business trades like a consumer platform - Indian consumer and telecom peers carry multiples well above RIL's consolidated ~22x P/E. The weak segment IS the discount.

## Risks
- Crude price swings can make O2C look great or terrible quarter to quarter - don't mistake one good quarter for a trend.
- Retail EBITDA actually fell 1.1% YoY on digital commerce investment - the "strong" segments also carry costs.
- Conglomerate breakups in India take years of regulatory and family-shareholding navigation.
